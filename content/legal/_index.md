## What this site is — and is not

The Value Forge is an independent clinical intelligence publication. It synthesises published evidence from NHS practice, policy, and international health systems for a professional audience, under a published production method and constraint system.

It is **not** an NHS service or NHS publication. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, funded by, or produced on behalf of the National Health Service, NHS England, or any NHS trust, integrated care board, or other health body. References to "the NHS" describe the health service as the publication's national subject, not any relationship with it. No NHS branding is used, and none may be inferred.

## Not medical or professional advice

Nothing on this site is medical advice, and nothing here creates a clinician–patient relationship.

The content is editorial synthesis of published research and policy, written for a professional readership. It is not a substitute for the judgement of a qualified clinician with the patient in front of them, and it must not be used to make decisions about the diagnosis, treatment, or care of any individual. **Never disregard, or delay seeking, professional medical advice because of something read here.** If you need medical help, contact your GP or NHS&nbsp;111; in an emergency, call&nbsp;999.

The same limit applies professionally: nothing here constitutes commissioning, procurement, financial, or legal advice. Evidence summarised on this site carries explicit labels — `Provisional` and `Contested` findings are labelled precisely because they are **not** a settled basis for decisions. The labels are part of the content; a reader who ignores them is not reading the publication it publishes.

## Accuracy, currency, and the limits of the promise

This publication is produced under a [published constraint system](/method/production/) designed to keep every claim tied to a verifiable source, and it maintains a public [corrections process](/corrections/). That is a discipline, not a guarantee. Evidence changes; studies are revised, superseded, and retracted; errors are possible despite the system built to prevent them.

Content therefore speaks **as of its stated date** and is provided **"as is" and "as available"**, without warranty of any kind, express or implied — including any warranty of accuracy, completeness, currency, or fitness for a particular purpose. Where content is found to be wrong, the remedy this publication offers is the one it promises: a dated, public correction.

## Privacy

This is a static site, and you can read all of it without giving us anything.

**We do not track you. There is no analytics of any kind** — no Google Analytics, no Plausible, Fathom, Matomo or equivalent, no tag manager, no advertising or social pixels, and no "anonymised" measurement either. **The site sets no cookies at all.** Search runs entirely inside your browser; typing a query sends nothing to us. Fonts are served from this site rather than a font network. There are no third-party scripts, trackers, or embedded images: the only external addresses on a normal page are ordinary links you can choose to click.

That is a deliberate position, not an oversight, and it is checkable — the site's full source is public.

**Who does touch anything, and what they get:**

- **Netlify** hosts the site and provides its DNS. Serving a page necessarily involves your IP address and browser reaching their servers, and they process standard server logs under [Netlify's privacy policy](https://www.netlify.com/privacy/). We do not receive, request, or store those logs.
- **GoDaddy** is the domain registrar and nothing more. The domain's nameservers are Netlify's, so no visit to this site — and not even a DNS lookup for it — reaches GoDaddy. They hold the registration record, not your data.
- **Google** processes feedback-form submissions, and only if you choose to send one. The form is not yet live; while it is not, nothing of Google's loads on any page here.
- **MailerLite** stores your email address, and only if you choose to subscribe.

If you never subscribe and never send feedback, the complete record of you held by this publication is nothing at all.

**The email edition.** If you subscribe, we hold the email address you give us, and nothing else. The lawful basis is your consent, given by subscribing — UK direct-marketing rules require an opt-in for email of this kind, and subscribing is that opt-in. The address is used solely to send you this publication: never sold, rented, or shared for anyone else's marketing, and never used to profile you. There is no subscriber list at all until you join one.

Emails are sent using [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/legal/privacy-policy), which stores and sends on our behalf as our **data processor**, under their terms. That arrangement governs what MailerLite does with the data; it does not transfer any responsibility away from this publication. We remain the controller, and requests about your data are ours to answer.

**Unsubscribing and erasure are two different things, and you can ask for either.** Every email carries a working unsubscribe link, which stops us sending to you. Erasure goes further and deletes your record entirely; ask through the [feedback route](/feedback/), including the address you subscribed with so we can find it, and we will action it in MailerLite. We keep your address only while you are subscribed.

**Feedback.** Collected through an embedded Google Form; anything you submit there is processed by Google under [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

**The form has no email field, and cannot identify you.** No account, no registration, no address, no name — we deliberately do not ask, because we do not reply and an address collected for a reply that never comes is data held for no reason. So unless you type identifying detail into the free-text box yourself, **we hold no personal data about you from the form at all**, and there is nothing arising from it to request, correct, or erase. Please don't type it in; it is not wanted and will be deleted.

We do not write back. Where something sent to us changes what we do, we may record the theme and the outcome, anonymised — most submissions will not appear. We never publish your identity or any identifying detail. The full handling is on the [feedback page](/feedback/) and in the [terms of use](/terms/).

**Your rights.** Under UK data protection law you may ask what we hold about you, ask us to correct or erase it, or withdraw consent at any time. There is very little to ask about — an email address, or nothing at all — but the route is real: use the [feedback route](/feedback/) and we will action it. Using a processor does not move these obligations; they remain ours.

**This is the one thing the feedback disclaimer does not touch.** Elsewhere we say plainly that we do not reply to feedback and are not obliged to act on it. That is about suggestions and editorial judgement. It has no application to a data protection request: those are your rights in law, not requests for a favour, and they are answered whether or not we agree with anything else you sent.

**How to make one, given the form asks for no address.** Send it through the [feedback form](/feedback/), say plainly that it is a data protection request, and — for this one purpose — **include the email address you subscribed with**. We ask you not to put personal detail in that box for any other reason, but here it is necessary and deliberate: the mailing list is keyed on the address, so without it there is no record for us to find, and a request we cannot match is a request we cannot action. That address is used to answer your request and for nothing else, is not carried into the triage staging, and is deleted with the record.

**Data controller.** This publication is produced by an individual editor in a personal capacity; that editor is the data controller. The editor publishes anonymously at present ([why](/method/production/)), and a request that genuinely requires identifying the controller — a regulator's enquiry, or a rights request we cannot otherwise satisfy — will be answered rather than deflected.
