On 11 June 2026, NHS England published its first monthly corridor care dataset, drawn from urgent and emergency care daily situation reports. For May 2026, the data recorded an average of 2,940 patients per day receiving care in clinically inappropriate settings — a figure covering patients in corridors, waiting rooms, and other non-designated clinical areas across NHS acute trusts in England. This is the first time this volume has been reported at a national, publicly accessible level.

The British Geriatrics Society welcomed the publication, framing the data within the broader argument for sustained community investment. The BGS has consistently positioned corridor care as a downstream consequence of insufficient community capacity — delayed hospital discharges, inadequate step-down provision, and lack of frailty-appropriate pathways in acute settings. The publication of a national dataset converts what was previously a qualitative narrative into a monthly accountability metric: a figure that will be tracked, compared, and questioned over time.

The corridor care dataset is explicitly linked to NHS England's standard-tightening trajectory. The current requirement for corridor care to be avoided in all cases will be accompanied by threshold adjustments in 2027/28, including a reduction in the permitted waiting time before escalation from 45 to 30 minutes. The publication of data precedes the tightening of standards, creating a baseline year against which future improvement — or failure to improve — will be measured.

NHS England's Frailty Pathways Best Practice Guide (May 2026) specifies a minimum 70-hour Same Day Emergency Care capacity as the operational standard for frailty-appropriate acute provision. This is the clinical standard against which corridor care data will ultimately be assessed at trust level. Trust-level breakdowns from the corridor care dataset have not yet been published; when they are, they will for the first time enable a direct comparison between corridor care rates and frailty pathway compliance at the same institution.
