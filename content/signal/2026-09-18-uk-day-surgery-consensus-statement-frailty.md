A UK Delphi-consensus guideline from the Association of Anaesthetists, the British Association for Day Surgery and the Association of Paediatric Anaesthetists sets out recommendations covering patient selection, pre-assessment and optimisation for day surgery. It names frailty, obesity and obstructive sleep apnoea explicitly as comorbidities that must be actively assessed for their effect on suitability, rather than functioning as automatic exclusion criteria.

[Guidance] The significance of this consensus statement is less any single recommendation than what its existence marks: mainstream peri-operative guidance is now naming frailty explicitly as a factor to assess and manage, rather than treating it as an unstated reason to exclude a patient from day-case pathways by default. Read alongside this week's evidence that a formal US surgical-verification programme raised vulnerability assessment rates in older patients, the pattern across both is that deliberate, structured mechanisms — a consensus statement with named criteria, or a monitored verification process — are what move frailty-assessment practice, more than general awareness of frailty as a clinical issue.

For day-surgery services selecting patients living with frailty, this consensus statement is directly applicable UK guidance for building or auditing local selection criteria against.
