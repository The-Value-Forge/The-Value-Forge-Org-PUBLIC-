The Chartered Society of Physiotherapy's quarterly member survey, published 13 August 2026, found 79% of NHS physiotherapy staff say staffing is insufficient for patient need [Audit/Data]. 68% report recruitment freezes, up from 53% a year earlier; 37% report deleted posts; 30% report cuts to whole services; 77% say they cannot offer the rehabilitation quality patients need; and 65% lack access to required equipment.

NICE mandates a specific rehabilitation intensity for appropriate stroke and frailty populations — three hours a day, five days a week — and it is against that explicit standard that this survey's findings should be read. The data demonstrates a widening gap between what national guidance requires and the workforce capacity available to deliver it, based on the direct testimony of practising physiotherapy staff nationally rather than a single service's local vacancy data.

This is a national capacity signal bearing directly on whether stroke and frailty rehabilitation pathways generally can sustain NICE-mandated intensity. It is a warning against assuming any further pathway redesign or service scaling can be absorbed without corresponding investment in the generalist rehabilitation workforce needed to deliver it.
