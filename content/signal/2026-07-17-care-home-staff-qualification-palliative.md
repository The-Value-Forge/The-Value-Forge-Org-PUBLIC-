A German cluster randomised controlled trial (10 nursing homes, 119 decedents) evaluated a 40-hour palliative care qualification course for care-home nursing staff [Primary Study]. Homes in the intervention arm achieved higher rates of specialist outpatient palliative contact (19.0% versus 9.1% in control homes) and better pain-management follow-through (odds ratio 1.80).

As a cluster randomised trial, this offers stronger causal evidence than an observational comparison of trained versus untrained homes would provide, though the number of homes and decedents involved is modest.

The intervention offers a concrete, exportable model for improving end-of-life care within nursing homes without requiring additional specialist staffing: structured qualification of the existing nursing workforce, which this trial associates with better specialist linkage and pain control, and which could reduce avoidable hospital transfer at the end of life for care-home residents.
